Word Frequency Analysis

While sentence length shapes the rhythm of your writing, word choice determines its texture. Word frequency analysis reveals how your vocabulary patterns compare to everyday English usage, giving you a data-driven window into your lexical style.

Every writer has a unique vocabulary fingerprint. Some rely heavily on high-frequency function words and simple verbs; others reach for rarer, more precise terms. Neither approach is inherently better, but understanding where you fall on this spectrum is the first step toward intentional vocabulary development.

How does our word frequency analysis work? We compare every word you write against a reference corpus of contemporary American English containing over 4,000 of the most common words. Each word is assigned a frequency rank, and the cumulative distribution of those ranks across your text is plotted. The resulting curve tells you at a glance whether your vocabulary skews common or uncommon.

What you can learn from word frequency analysis:

  1. Discover whether you over-rely on a narrow set of common words
  2. See how your vocabulary breadth compares across different pieces of writing
  3. Track vocabulary growth over time as you read more and learn new words
  4. Identify opportunities to replace generic terms with more precise alternatives

One important note: a higher proportion of rare words does not automatically mean better writing. Technical and academic prose naturally uses lower-frequency vocabulary. Fiction and journalism often thrive on clear, accessible language. Context is everything, and our tool gives you the numbers without passing judgment.

Try pasting different types of writing into the analyzer and compare the curves. You might be surprised by how much your word frequency profile shifts between an email, a report, and a creative piece. Awareness is the first step toward control.
